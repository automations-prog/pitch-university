# ACA role-play objections — consumer lines and approved responses

Prepared for Patrick, 15 October 2026.

Every objection below is drawn from **3,979 analysed ACA calls** (Sept–Oct 2026). The consumer phrasings
are things real consumers actually said, transcribed from recordings — they are what the AI should say.
The responses are the approved wording from the ACA script, which is what agents should practise saying
back. Where a top agent handled it particularly well on a real call, their exact words are included too.

**"Seen on"** is the share of calls where the objection came up. **"Handled badly"** is how often agents
got it weak or missed it entirely — the higher that number, the more practice reps need on it.

A note on one limitation: the export behind these numbers contained **transferred calls only**, so these
frequencies describe objections agents *got past*. They do not tell us which objections lose calls. On
another vertical where we had the lost calls too, the pattern was that the most common objections were the
least dangerous and the brush-offs did the damage. Worth assuming the same here until we can measure it.

## The four you asked about

### “I don't want to change my insurance”
*Seen on 1.6% of calls · handled badly 20.0% of the time*

**What consumers actually say:**
- “I'm okay. I don't want to say nothing out right now.”
- “I don't want to speak to anybody because the last time I did, they changed my insurance.”
- “Now, this won't affect my plan, right? ... Like the United Healthcare gold plan.”

**Approved response:**

> You'd be the one to change anything, I can't do that. The purpose is simply to verify whether you still live in (STATE) and currently have no Medicare, Medicaid, or employer coverage.

**How top agents said it on real calls:**
- “No, only you have the power to change your insurance, sweetheart. Not me, not anybody else, just you.”
- “That's okay. You just let them know that that's not a problem. That only you have the power to do that.”

### “Is it money? A card? Help with bills?”
*Seen on 3.3% of calls · handled badly 14.4% of the time*

**What consumers actually say:**
- “Are you talking about. Money? Any insurance or something like that? I don't know what?”
- “Do I have to pay anything for anything?”
- “It's something that costs money. So it's like, if it costs money, I can't do it because I have none.”

**Approved response:**

> Since it's confidential between the licensed agent and yourself, once I verify everything you can go ahead and ask the licensed agent. Now, do you still not receive any Medicare, Medicaid or work insurance?

**How top agents said it on real calls:**
- “No, this is a free government provided allowance. I don't have any questions for you. Nothing personal, nothing financial.”
- “I'm not sure. I don't want to promise you something and then it not happen. I honestly don't know. But if you've read something, that may be accurate.”

### “How did you get my information?”
*Seen on 2.0% of calls · handled badly 26.9% of the time*

**What consumers actually say:**
- “And how did you get my number?”
- “So who told you to get in contact with me?”
- “I just got my number changed to this number right here... I never talked to anyone on this number.”

**Approved response:**

> I do have you here for a reason — we are actually only calling those that may qualify for a free subsidy with the Affordable Care Act. Now, you do not receive Medicare or Medicaid or any work insurance, correct?

**How top agents said it on real calls:**
- “I don't have very much information. I just have your name and your state. This comes through the Affordable Care act or it can come through somebody trying to see if they qualify for something.”
- “It's national. It's in every state. It's all 50 states. It's the Affordable Care Act I don't have any of your information except your name and the state you're in.”

## The pattern that matters more than any single rebuttal

Every approved ACA rebuttal has the same three beats:

1. **Acknowledge** what they said.
2. **Answer briefly** — one or two sentences, never a speech.
3. **Go straight back** to the verification: *still in (STATE), still no Medicare, Medicaid or work insurance?*

Notice that every response above ends on a question. That is deliberate — whoever asks the last question
decides where the call goes next. If the AI is built to reward a rebuttal that ends on a statement, agents
will learn to hand control back to the consumer.

For the role play, the two behaviours worth scoring are **whether the agent returns to the verification**
and **whether they do it without pausing**. Those matter more than word-for-word recall of the rebuttal.

## Three things worth adding to the role play beyond objections

From the same 3,979 calls, these are what agents actually get wrong — more often than they fumble a rebuttal:

| Behaviour | How often |
|---|--:|
| Transferred without the consumer saying okay | **16.5%** |
| Second M/M/W confirmation skipped entirely | **33.8%** |
| A required question asked, then talked over without waiting for the answer | **~25%** |

A consumer in the role play who sometimes **doesn't answer** — stays silent, or starts talking over the
agent — would train the pause better than any rebuttal drill. And a consumer who occasionally gives a
vague answer (“I think so”, “my wife handles that”) forces the agent to actually resolve it rather than
accept it.
